Methodology

Both models were trained on TF-IDF (NMF) / count-vectorized (LDA) tokens after lemmatization and stopword filtering. Coherence uses the C_v metric on the top-10 words per topic. See the Dataset page for the full pipeline.

MODEL - LATENT DIRICHLET ALLOCATION

LDA results

A probabilistic generative model: each document is a mixture of topics, each topic a distribution over words.

How LDA Works

Imagine you have a giant pile of documents. Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) assumes that each document is just a mixture of different topics (e.g., 60% Sports, 40% Politics), and each topic is just a mixture of words (e.g., Sports uses "ball" and "game" a lot).

LDA works backward. It looks at the words in the documents and asks: "What hidden topics, and what word mixtures for those topics, would most likely generate these exact documents?" It uses probability to group words that frequently appear together into "topics", without ever knowing what the texts actually mean!

  • Input: Raw text documents.
  • Assumption 1: Documents are probability distributions over topics.
  • Assumption 2: Topics are probability distributions over words.
  • Output: The hidden topics and the percentage of each topic in every document.

MODEL - NON-NEGATIVE MATRIX FACTORIZATION

NMF results

A deterministic linear-algebra factorization of the TF-IDF matrix into non-negative topic and document components.

How NMF Works

Think of Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) as a giant math puzzle. You start with a massive grid (a matrix) where rows are documents, columns are words, and the numbers represent how often a word appears in a document.

NMF mathematically squishes this giant matrix into two much smaller matrices that, when multiplied together, recreate the original grid. The "Non-negative" rule means we can't use negative numbers. This forces the algorithm to learn building blocks (topics) that strictly add up to form a document, making the topics highly interpretable!

Model interpretation

What the numbers mean, in practice.

1

NMF produces sharper, more interpretable topics on this dataset — its top words carry higher weight concentration per topic.

2

LDA distributes probability more evenly across documents, capturing softer thematic overlaps but at the cost of coherence.

3

For short, opinionated reviews with TF-IDF weighting, NMF's non-negative factorization aligns well with human topic judgments.

4

LDA remains preferable when documents are longer, more mixed, or when a generative probabilistic interpretation is needed.

Dataset & preprocessing

The Amazon customer-review corpus used across every experiment on this site.

About the Data

We use the Amazon Product Data dataset (He and McAuley, 2016), specifically the Electronics 5-core subset. This dataset contains millions of reviews from May 1996 to July 2014. The "5-core" designation means it only includes items and users that have at least 5 reviews each, ensuring a dense, high-quality corpus for NLP tasks. It's a standard benchmark for sentiment analysis and topic modeling due to its rich, opinionated text.

Preprocessing pipeline

Steps applied identically for LDA and NMF (except the vectorizer).

  1. Lowercased text
  2. Removed HTML entities and URLs
  3. Removed English stopwords (NLTK) + custom domain list
  4. Lemmatized using WordNet
  5. Kept tokens with length >= 3
  6. TF-IDF (NMF) / Count Vectorizer (LDA) with min_df=5, max_df=0.9

Source

Amazon Product Data (He & McAuley, UCSD, 2016). We use the Electronics 5-core subset for reproducibility. See the original release page.
